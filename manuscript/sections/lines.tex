\section{The line graph in dimension six}
\label{sec:line}
This section determines the chromatic number of the line graph
\(\Gamma_6\), whose vertices are the \(63\) nonzero vectors of
\(\F^6\) with two distinct vectors adjacent when their dot product
vanishes.  We proceed in two steps.  First, we bound the size of any
independent set in \(\Gamma_6\) by a weighted count on even- and
odd-weight vectors; this yields the lower bound
\(\chi(\Gamma_6)\ge 12\).  Second, we exhibit an explicit
\(12\)-coloring of the \(63\) nonzero vectors, verified by an
independent checker, which gives the matching upper bound.  Together
these prove \(\chi(\Gamma_6)=12\).  The lower bound is the
substantial combinatorial input; the upper bound is a finite
certificate recorded in Section~\ref{sec:computational}.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:weighted}
An independent set in $\Gamma_6$ containing $t$ even-weight vectors and $o$
odd-weight vectors satisfies $3t+4o\leq19$. Its cardinality is at most six;
cardinality six requires $(t,o)=(5,1)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $j=(1,1,1,1,1,1)$. The even-weight vectors lie in $j^\perp$, whose
restricted form has radical $\Span(j)$ and rank four. If $o=0$, the Gram
matrix of the selected vectors is $J_t+I_t$, of rank $t$ for even $t$ and
$t-1$ for odd $t$. Thus $t\leq5$.

If $o>0$, fix an odd selected vector $a$. For each even selected vector $e$,
the vector $z_e=e+a$ belongs to $a^\perp$. These vectors are orthonormal and
span a nondegenerate $t$-space $A$. The ambient space $a^\perp$ is
nondegenerate of dimension five. The span $D$ of the vectors $u+a$, over odd
selected vectors $u$, is totally isotropic and orthogonal to $A$ inside
$a^\perp$. Hence $2\dim D\leq5-t$. The odd selected vectors are distinct
members of $a+D$, so $o\leq2^{\dim D}$. Consequently $o\leq4$ for $t=0,1$,
$o\leq2$ for $t=2,3$, and $o\leq1$ for $t=4,5$. These bounds prove both claims.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:lines}
$\chi(\Gamma_6)=12$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
There are $31$ nonzero even-weight and $32$ odd-weight vectors. Their total
weight, using weights three and four respectively, is $221$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:weighted}, every color class has weight at most $19$, giving
$\chi(\Gamma_6)\geq\lceil221/19\rceil=12$.
For the upper bound, interpret an integer $x$ as the vector whose coordinate
$i$ is its $i$th binary digit. The certificate
\path{results/line-coloring-12.json} partitions the $63$ nonzero vectors
into $12$ classes. The independent standard-library checker
\path{develop/check_line_chromatic_exact.py} checks all $1953$ pairs and
verifies that each of the $961$ orthogonality edges has distinct colors.
\end{proof}

For completeness, the twelve color classes, in the same binary integer
coordinates, are displayed below. They partition $\{1,\ldots,63\}$, and
their within-class dot products can be checked directly.
\[
\begin{gathered}
\{8,13,29,42,47\},\quad \{2,26,38,62,63\},\quad
\{20,37,39,45,48\},\\
\{6,11,18,34,53,60\},\quad \{15,30,46,49,59\},\quad
\{32,40,50,51,54\},\\
\{14,19,24,41,52\},\quad \{12,27,43,55,57,58\},\quad
\{4,7,28,31,36\},\\
\{16,21,23,56,61\},\quad \{10,22,25,35,44\},\quad
\{1,3,5,9,17,33\}.
\end{gathered}
\]

Any $12$-coloring has a class of size six, since $12\cdot5<63$.
By the lemma it
consists of an odd vector $a$ and five even vectors $e$. The vectors $a$ and
$e+a$ form an orthonormal basis. An isometry therefore maps this class to
$\{1,3,5,9,17,33\}$. Fixing this class reduces the remaining question to an
$11$-coloring of the induced graph on the other $57$ vectors.
The direct Boolean encoding uses $627$ variables and $11\,772$ clauses:
each vertex has exactly one of eleven colors, and each of the $780$ edges
forbids a shared color. The solver supplies a satisfying assignment;
adjoining the fixed class gives the displayed certificate. The proof of
the upper bound depends on the independent certificate check, rather than
on the solver's correctness.
