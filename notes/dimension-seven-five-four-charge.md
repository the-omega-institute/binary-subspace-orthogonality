# Four necessary normal forms for the pure-five and four-even defect profile

**Theorem.** A nineteen-coloring of Gamma_7 with characteristic class
size four and outside profile (k5,k6;A,B,C)=(5,4;6,2,8), if one
exists, admits one of the four marked normal forms below. The pure
five-even defect contains exactly one even hole point, and each of
the six pure even heptads contains exactly one of the other holes.

This is a written necessary reduction, checked by exact local
catalogs and normalization controls. None of the four normal forms
is asserted realizable or excluded. The current totals remain
nineteen of forty-six 5+6 and nine of forty-eight 6+6+6 raw profiles
excluded, leaving twenty-seven and thirty-nine unexcluded. Exact
dimension-seven chromatic numbers and characteristic sizes four
through eight remain open, with lower bound nineteen.

## The existing charge-member lemma removes the apparent exceptional branch

Put E=z-perp and C_z={z,z+e,z+f,z+g}, with h=e+f+g. The eight
pure odd heptads leave the nonzero points of a Lagrangian M by the
[written eight-spread theorem](dimension-seven-nineteen-obstruction.md).
The remaining odd projections partition into e,f,g, the two D6
projections a,d, and the two mixed projections b,c. D5 is pure even.
Write p for its five-even sum and q for the D6 four-even sum.

Partition charge and the quadratic identity give

```text
h=a+d+b+c,       p+q=b+c,       p dot(b+c)=0.
```

The four-even pairings also give p dot a=p dot d=0. These identities
alone would force p into M when a,d,b+c span M but would leave an
apparent exception when that span has dimension two. There is no
need to treat that exception separately: the existing
[charge-member lemma](dimension-seven-two-six-profiles.md) applies
to the D6 class itself. Its charge q+a+d is one of a,d, so q is
the other. Relabel the two odd projections so that

```text
q=a,       w6=d,       p=a+b+c in M,       h=p+d.
```

Thus both even sums belong to M in every case, including the
dependent span. This is an application of the earlier lemma, not
a new charge-member theorem. The quadratic condition then holds
automatically by isotropy of M and supplies no further exclusion.

For completeness, the four-even sum conclusion also has a direct
span proof. Let W be the nondegenerate four-even span. Its Gram
matrix is I+J over F_2, whose square is I. The nonzero vector
r=a+d belongs to W-perp; so does a+q. Since M is isotropic and
q belongs to W, (a+q) dot r=0. In the nondegenerate plane W-perp,
the perpendicular line of r is span(r). Consequently a+q is
either zero or r, giving q=a or q=d. This argument does not first
assume q belongs to M.

## The zero five-even sum must remain a separate case

The five-even Gram matrix has rank four and radical generated by
the all-ones coefficient vector. Its vector sum p pairs to zero
with every member. If p is zero, the five members span a
nondegenerate four-space. If p is nonzero, they are linearly
independent and span p-perp, with radical span(p). Neither case
permits treating p as the nonzero sum of a four-even class.

The seven even points of M form a clique. D6 and both mixed labels
are forbidden there by their odd projections in M. The characteristic
and pure odd labels are also unavailable. The only labels are the
six pure even heptads and the pure-five defect. Each such class
meets M at most once, so all seven meet M exactly once. If t is
the pure-five hole point and p is nonzero, then t differs from p:
p is perpendicular to every member, whereas t pairs to one with
the other four members.

After choosing D5, D6 and both mixed even sextets disjointly,
42 even points remain, including the six holes other than t.
They must partition into six pure even heptads, each containing
one of those six holes and avoiding t. This is the correct remaining
covering problem; the preceding seven-anchored-heptad certificate
does not apply to it.

## Four exhaustive marked normal forms

The points a,b,c are distinct and nonzero. Their sum p is either
zero or, if they are independent, different from a,b,c. In the
second case p is either d or a characteristic projection. When p
is characteristic, h=p+d is nonzero, different from d, and is
either a or outside {a,d}. In the latter case h is a mixed
projection, as the normalization below shows. This gives exactly
the four cases:

| Case | (p,q) | D6 odd projections (a,d) | Mixed projections {b,c} | Characteristic projections | h |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Z: p=0 | (0,15) | (15,48) | {3,12} | {51,60,63} | 48 |
| H: h=0 | (12,3) | (3,12) | {48,63} | {15,51,60} | 0 |
| D: nonzero h in D6 | (63,48) | (48,15) | {3,12} | {51,60,63} | 48 |
| G: nonzero h in a mixed class | (12,3) | (3,48) | {51,60} | {12,15,63} | 60 |

Here z=127 and M={0,3,12,15,48,51,60,63}. The odd line vector
with projection x is z+x. The two mixed classes may be exchanged;
the five- and six-point defects remain distinguished.

These normalizations follow from basis construction:

- In Z, a=b+c and d is outside span(b,c). Send (b,c,d) to (3,12,48).
- In H, d=a+b+c and a,d,b are independent. Send (a,d,b) to (3,12,48).
- In D, h=a gives d=b+c, with a outside span(b,c). Send (b,c,a) to (3,12,48).
- In G, a,p,d are independent; otherwise d=a+p would give h=a.
  Send (a,p,d) to (3,12,48). Since b+c=a+p and neither mixed
  point is a,p,d, their unordered pair is {a+d,p+d}, mapping to
  {51,60}.

Each ordered basis of M extends to a symplectic basis of E, and
the resulting isometry extends to the original dot space by fixing
z. This proves a universal marked reduction, without assuming
transitivity on complete colorings.

have nonzero sum in M, and 2,688 have sum outside M. The charge
reduction removes the last family from this specified profile.

fixed-M odd-role assignments with the selected D6 sum, covering
