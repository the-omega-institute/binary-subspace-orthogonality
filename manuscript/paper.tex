\begin{abstract}
Let $\OG_n^*$ be the graph whose vertices are the nonzero subspaces of
$\F^n$, with two distinct vertices adjacent when every vector of one is
orthogonal to every vector of the other, and let $\Gamma_n$ be its
induced subgraph on the one-dimensional subspaces. We prove
\[
  \omega(\OG_n^*)=\max\bigl\{\,n,\; N(\lfloor n/2\rfloor)+(n \bmod 2)\,\bigr\}
  \qquad (n\ge 1),
\]
where $N(r)=\sum_{d=1}^{r}\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{r}{d}_2$ is the number of nonzero
subspaces of $\F^r$. The proof is algebraic: it sums the radicals of an
arbitrary clique and passes to the resulting orthogonal quotient. In
dimension six we show, with computer-assisted certificates, that
$\chi(\OG_6^*)=15$ and $\chi(\Gamma_6)=12$, so the full subspace graph
needs exactly three more colors than its line subgraph. The lower bound
for $\Gamma_6$ comes from a weighted independent-set bound; both upper
bounds are explicit colorings verified by independent checkers, and the
clique theorem and the $15$-coloring are also formalized in Lean,
with native evaluation for the finite checks in the latter. We
further show that no proper coloring of $\OG_6^*$ with the prescribed
clique colors is invariant under the stabilizer of the fixed totally
isotropic $3$-space, and we record a dimension reduction for $n=7$,
whose chromatic number remains open.
\end{abstract}
