\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Let \(V_n=\F^n\) denote the \(n\)-dimensional vector space over the
field with two elements, equipped with the standard nondegenerate
bilinear form
\[
\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i .
\]
The \emph{orthogonality graph} \(\OG_n^*\) has as its vertices the
nonzero linear subspaces of \(V_n\).  Two distinct vertices \(U,W\)
are adjacent when every vector of \(U\) is orthogonal to every vector
of \(W\), that is, \(U\subseteq W^\perp\).  Its induced subgraph on
the one-dimensional subspaces is denoted by \(\Gamma_n\).  Since each
line of \(V_n\) contains a unique nonzero vector, \(\Gamma_n\) can
equivalently be described as the graph on the \(2^n-1\) nonzero
vectors of \(V_n\) with two distinct vectors adjacent when their dot
product is zero.

The graph \(\OG_n^*\) belongs to a natural family of graphs arising
from finite classical polar spaces.  Its line subgraph \(\Gamma_n\) is
a finite-field analogue of the Kneser graph: two lines are adjacent
when their representative vectors are orthogonal, rather than when
their supports are disjoint.  The chromatic number of the Kneser
graph was determined by Lov\'asz \cite{Lovasz1979} using topological
methods; the clique and chromatic numbers of polarity graphs and
their relatives have been studied extensively in finite geometry and
algebraic graph theory
\cite{BrouwerCohenNeumaier1989,GodsilRoyle2001,BrouwerHaemers2012}.
The subspace graph \(\OG_n^*\) refines the line graph by admitting
higher-dimensional subspaces as additional vertices, and it is this
enlargement that makes the chromatic problem substantially different.

\subsection*{Motivation and background}

The graph \(\OG_n^*\) arises naturally in two independent contexts.
On the combinatorial side, it is the graph of pairwise orthogonal
subspaces of a finite vector space, a natural object in the theory of
polar spaces \cite{Cameron1991}.  On the algebraic side, it is
isomorphic to the annihilating-ideal graph of a family of finite
local rings introduced by Nikandish \cite{Nikandish2026}, and thus
provides a bridge between the theory of graphs over finite fields
and the study of graphs associated with commutative rings
\cite{Beck1988,AndersonNaseer1993,AndersonLivingston1999,
BehboodiRakeei2011a,BehboodiRakeei2011b}.

The bridge is as follows.  For
\[
R_n=\frac{\F[x_1,\dots,x_n,z]}{(z^2,\ zx_i,\ x_ix_j\,(i\ne j),\
x_i^2-z)},
\]
the map \(U\mapsto (z)+U\) is a bijection between the subspaces of
\(V_n\) and the nonzero proper ideals of \(R_n\), and
\(I_UI_W=0\) if and only if \(U\perp W\) \cite{Nikandish2026}.  The
zero subspace corresponds to the ideal \((z)\), which is a universal
vertex, so
\[
\chi(\AG(R_n))=\chi(\OG_n^*)+1,\qquad
\omega(\AG(R_n))=\omega(\OG_n^*)+1 .
\]
In particular, any statement about \(\OG_n^*\) translates directly
into a statement about \(\AG(R_n)\), and conversely.

\begin{problem}\label{prob:clique}
Determine the exact value of \(\omega(\OG_n^*)\) for every \(n\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{prob:chromatic}
Determine \(\chi(\OG_n^*)\) for \(n\ge6\) and investigate the relation
between \(\chi(\OG_n^*)\) and \(\omega(\OG_n^*)\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{prob:infinite}
Determine whether there exist infinitely many \(n\ge1\) with
\(\chi(\OG_n^*)>\omega(\OG_n^*)\).
\end{problem}

\subsection*{Results}

Put
\[
N(r)=\sum_{d=1}^{r}{r\brack d}_2,
\]
where \({r\brack d}_2\) is the Gaussian binomial coefficient, and set
\(N(0)=0\).  Our results are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clique-formula}
\omega(\OG_n^*)=\max\bigl\{n,\ N(\lfloor n/2\rfloor)+(n\bmod 2)\bigr\}
\qquad(n\ge1),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chromatic-six}
\chi(\OG_6^*)=15,\qquad \chi(\Gamma_6)=12 .
\end{equation}
The first formula answers Problem~\ref{prob:clique} in all
dimensions.  The second answers the dimension-six case of
Problem~\ref{prob:chromatic} and quantifies the separation between
coloring the line graph and coloring the full subspace graph: in
dimension six, passing from lines to all nonzero subspaces increases
the chromatic number by exactly three.

The clique argument is algebraic: it sums the radicals of an arbitrary
clique and passes to the resulting orthogonal quotient.  The
full-graph chromatic upper bound uses an explicit finite certificate,
while the line-graph equality follows from a weighted independent-set
bound and a checked \(12\)-coloring.  The verification details and the
scope of the Lean formalization are collected in
Section~\ref{sec:computational}.

For small \(n\), the exact values of \(\omega\) and \(\chi\) are known
and are recorded in Table~\ref{tab:small-values} below.  They show
that \(\chi(\OG_n^*)>\omega(\OG_n^*)\) from \(n=4\) onward, so the
orthogonality graph is not weakly perfect; Equation
\eqref{eq:chromatic-six} extends this gap to dimension six, where it
reaches three.

The paper is organized as follows.  Section~\ref{sec:clique} contains
the proof of \eqref{eq:clique-formula}.
Section~\ref{sec:line} treats the line graph \(\Gamma_6\) and
establishes \(\chi(\Gamma_6)=12\).
Section~\ref{sec:chromatic} proves \(\chi(\OG_6^*)=15\).
Section~\ref{sec:geometry} describes the geometry of the precoloring
and records the obstructions to a structural formula.
Section~\ref{sec:higher} states the remaining open problems and a
dimension reduction for \(n=7\).
Section~\ref{sec:computational} collects the computational
certificates and the Lean formalization.
